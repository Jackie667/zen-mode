Four channels

One story, told differently on each platform

The deep read

WeChat Official Account

Longer articles, room by room, for students and parents in China: the render, every item, and the price (£550 for the Gaming room), so parents can decide too.

On launch day I asked six site teams to repost it on their own channels.

Original WeChat article (in Chinese).

Discovery

Xiaohongshu

Searchable notes with strong covers. On Xiaohongshu people search first and save for later, so a note travels when people save it.

This post: 1,564 views against a usual 800 to 900, 165 likes and 23 enquiries.

Launch day, 1 June 2022: the article shared with six site teams to repost.

The look

Instagram and TikTok

Carousels and short video for students already in the UK. The cover asks a question, "Which room are you?", because a question gets comments and shares; a list doesn't.

From post to booking

A short path from interest to a paid add-on

1 · Join

A WeChat interest group kept leads warm until booking opened. (QR code blurred.)

2 · Book and add a theme

On the website, the theme is added at checkout.

3 · Revenue£400–550

per room, sold as a paid add-on. "Only a few left" on every post gave people a reason to book now. The campaign created a new revenue line, not only reach.

What I'd do differently

A spike is a start, not a result

Plan the follow-up

Attention fades in days. I'd have a series ready, one room a week, to hold it.

Track through to bookings

Tag every enquiry by source, so I can show which channel turned interest into tenancies.

Put more into TikTok

It brought the most enquiries (35). The follow-up series would have started there.

What this campaign shows

Find what's worth sharing, then help the right people share it

A story, not a listing

Partners as amplifiers
