Foundation — The Brand System

How do two buildings, two cities, two launch years and a dozen different suppliers all sound like the same person?

Built Before the First Supplier Was Briefed
  • A wordmark drawn from a single, constant stroke width — every clear-space, minimum-size and misuse rule documented so no supplier ever redraws it by eye
  • A two-colour system (crimson + olive) with contrast ratios worked out in advance, so agencies stop guessing what "on-brand" means
  • A voice built around one real constraint of student housing: every sentence is read by both the 18-year-old choosing the room and the parent paying for it
  • A "say this, not that" reference table, so seasonal copywriters and social interns don't have to re-learn the tone from a brief
  • Full specs for every touchpoint the leasing campaign would actually use — lamppost banners, billboards, social formats, merchandise — documented before the first supplier was briefed

JSU's Take

"Urgency only works if it's true. A flash sale on a building that's already full reads as desperate; a flash sale on the last dozen rooms before term starts reads as fact. The discount didn't fill the building — the first two phases did. This one just closed it."

What Marketing Can't Fix

  • Location fundamentals. No campaign compensates for a 40-minute walk to campus with no direct transport link.
  • Price-to-market fit. If the rent doesn't match what the room offers relative to competitors, urgency tactics just accelerate the discovery of that gap.
  • University partnerships. Nomination agreements and student-union relationships open a channel no ad budget replicates.
  • Product quality. The showroom and sleepover events only work if the finished room matches what was staged.
  • Word of mouth. The tenants who move in during Phase 3 become next year's Phase 1 — the campaign that matters most is the one you don't run.
